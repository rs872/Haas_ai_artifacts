Locked-In Day Briefing
Turns a vague task list into a time-stamped, anxiety-aware plan he can listen to while walking. Built on GTD (David Allen), 5W1H, and capacity planning. Order everything by priority, high level first, details after.

Trigger
He says plan my day, plan my week, lock me in, brief me, or shares a to-do list or My Day view that looks vague. Offer this proactively when he starts planning.

Method (do in this order)
Sync the clock. Print system date and time first. Everything is relative to now. Drop any block that has already passed and re-plan from the current time.
Gather sources before analyzing. Notion tracker (Tasks, Projects, Task Journal), Google Calendar (all sub-calendars, today through the next trip or deadline), Gmail for travel and logistics, memory for standing priorities and rules. Read-only until he approves writes.
Capacity versus demand. Estimate minutes per task, sum them, and compare with real focus time after fixed events. State that estimates are guesses. Output a demand versus capacity chart.
GTD clarify each task. For every item write: project it belongs to, next physical action (a verb he can do with his hands), context (where and with what tool), time needed, energy level, and a definition of done. Flag items that are thoughts, not actions.
5W1H and logistics. Why (project), Who, What, Where, When, How. Then check collisions, missing buffers, tasks that need business hours, tasks needing wifi versus offline, time zones, travel days, and meals.
Find the gaps. Hidden deadlines caused by travel, the scariest task missing from the list, duplicate tasks that are really one task, answers he already built but is about to rebuild, stale done items still checked.
Choose 3 or 4 outcomes for the day. Everything else gets a named date, goes to dead time (plane, walking), or is archived. Give a drop order for when time slips. Protect: the anxiety-driving task first, then the high-leverage sends, then logistics.
Scene-by-scene day plan. Exact times, location, action, and done criteria. Use walking time for hands-free items (voice texts, listening, deciding dinner).
Guardrails. Plan for interruptions he already knows will come: forgotten lunch, dinner, texts from Sakari (and a reminder to text her unprompted). Add a Parking Lot rule for new ideas, a spiral rule (if the pull to rethink the whole future shows up, write one line and restart the timer), a checking-loop rule, a three-part start (timer on, file open, first sentence typed), and name the dread once then do the next small step. Tools, not treatment. Mention a clinician if loops dominate his days.
Debrief. End with 3 questions: what did I send or book, what is the one thing I carry forward, what did I park and when does it come back.
Deliver. PDF (not markdown) plus a short audio briefing in chapters (local TTS or equivalent; no em dashes in the script). Then offer to write the plan to Google Calendar, using the Google Calendar connector only, never Apple Calendar. Put blocks on his Berkeley calendar where existing blocks live unless he says otherwise. Put the next physical action, context, and done criteria in each event description. Flag conflicts with other people's events instead of moving them.
Limitations section, research-paper style. List estimates, unverified assumptions, things not found, hallucination risks, confounds, and further exploration.
Format rules
Show every deadline in both UTC and PT, and verify day of week against system time.
Never invent numbers or figures. State assumptions and ask.
No em dashes and no it's not X, it's Y constructions.
Remove any Lean Six Sigma mention from any resume he sends.
Do not draft his resume text; his loop is the CMG resume tool, Grokbot, then Stephanie at CMG. Claude holds accountability and process.
Periodically remind him about the Drucker check (economics, psychology, sociology, politics, ethics).
Keep Claude out of the middle of his work blocks: Claude is for the briefing and the evening debrief.
Output order
Bottom line, four outcomes, ranked gaps, dated deadlines, task table (action, context, time), schedule, guardrails, debrief, limitations, further exploration.
